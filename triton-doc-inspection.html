Leg Wires Inspection Procedure

I wrote this so a brand-new electrical member could pick it up with no harness experience: survey every motor-to-motor segment, test every harness against ten pass/fail checks, and earn solder qualification before touching the robot. Tables A and B are the logs the team actually filled in on the robot.

1. Purpose & Scope

This procedure surveys every motor-to-motor CAN/power segment, inspects every existing harness, and qualifies members to build replacements. The data recorded in Tables A and B drives the replacement BOM in Section 7.

  • In scope: CAN signal leads, XT30 power leads, and motor-side connectors on the two leg chains (motors 1–5, 6–10).
  • Out of scope: PDB, Jetson, ESP32 and battery wiring (see Gen 1 spec sheet, Torso Electronics).
  • Rule: Record what you observe, not what you expect. If unsure, write “?” and flag it in the Notes column.

2. Safety & Required Equipment

The robot runs on a 12S LiPo (44.4 V nominal, 50.4 V full charge). The E-stop cuts only the 48 V motor rail, so it is not a lockout.

2.1 Pre-work checklist

  • ☐ Battery disconnected at the XT90-S connector and physically removed from the enclosure
  • ☐ Robot supported on the bench (legs cannot fold or fall when a harness is pulled)
  • ☐ No bench power supply connected to the PDB
  • ☐ Soldering station on a heat mat, fume extractor on, safety glasses worn
  • ☐ Lead or senior member signed off before first solder joint

3.3 Wire color caution

CAN pairs on this robot are not color-consistent: green/white, blue/brown and red/black pairs have all been seen on S1 plugs. Never assume red = +48 V on a signal lead. Identify by connector, then confirm with the multimeter. New builds standardize on one CAN pair color (BOM item 7).

4. Procedure A — Motor-to-Motor Segment Survey

One row in Table A per segment x–y. Complete Procedure A before removing any harness in Procedure B, so lengths are measured with motors in their installed positions.

4.1 Steps

  • Find (or add) the row for segment x–y in Table A (e.g. 7–8).
  • Locate the starting motor by its yellow label x. Record its connector Ref (P1 / P2 / S1, Section 3.2) in Conn @ x.
  • Locate the ending motor y. Record its connector Ref in Conn @ y.
  • Measure L_min: with joints in neutral, route a tape along the path a harness would take (along the frame, not through air) from connector face x to connector face y. Record in cm.
  • Measure L_max: move every joint between x and y slowly through its full range of motion by hand. Record the longest connector-to-connector path seen.
  • Record Slack = L_max − L_min (cm). If the path rubs an edge or pinches at any joint angle, note it.
  • Compute L_cut = L_min + Slack +4 cm (2 cm per end for strip, solder and strain relief).

5. Procedure B — Harness Removal, Tagging & Inspection

Every harness gets a letter ID, is tested against B1–B10, and ends in exactly one pile. A harness passes only if B1–B8 all pass.

5.1 Removal and tagging

  • Write your name in the next free row of Table B. Your harness ID is that row’s letter (e.g. “C”).
  • Before unplugging, wrap masking tape around the harness and write the ID in Sharpie.
  • Put a second tape tag on the robot at each motor the harness connected to: ID + segment (e.g. “C / 7–8”). This records which harnesses were installed and where.
  • Unplug by the connector housing, never by the wires. Depress the latch on S1 plugs first.
  • In Table B, set Removed from to the segment, or “Loose” if the harness came from a bin.
  • Log the CAN lead and the power lead as two rows under the same ID. A harness with only one lead gets one row.

6. Procedure C — Soldering Qualification (Practice)

A member is qualified to build replacement harnesses after 3 accepted joints per connector type. Practice parts come from the shop stock, not the robot.

6.1 Steps

  • Watch at least one XT30 soldering video from the table below before your first joint.
  • Solder 3 Amass XT30UW-F connectors to 16 AWG silicone wire (pin 1 = +; check the polarity mark on the housing).
  • Slide heat shrink on before soldering; shrink after the joint cools.
  • Have a qualified member inspect each joint against 6.2 and run B6 (pull) and B8 (continuity).
  • Repeat for the S1 CAN connector: crimp 3 JST GH housings (GHR-02V-S) with SSHL-002T-P0.2 contacts using the PA-09 pliers. GH contacts accept 26–30 AWG only, so practice on 26 AWG wire. Check that each contact latches in the housing and does not pull out by hand (log as C1–C3 in Table C).
  • If the lead approves the splice method (Section 8.1), solder a 22 AWG CAN trunk to a short 26–28 AWG GH pigtail and cover it with adhesive-lined heat shrink. Practice 3 of these.
  • Log every attempt in Table C, including rejects.

7. Bill of Materials — Replacement Harnesses

Quantities are filled after Tables A and B are complete and reviewed. Each Qty basis below states how to derive the number; round up to the vendor pack size.

7.1 Derivation rules

  • N_seg = number of Table A segments whose installed harness is Replace, Maintenance, or missing.
  • N_P1, N_S1 = connector ends of each type across those segments (from Conn @ x and Conn @ y), counted per gender. Add N_P2 once that connector is identified.
  • L_pwr = Σ L_cut of those segments. Item 8 is a bonded red/black pair, so one length covers both conductors.
  • L_can = Σ L_cut of those segments (twisted pair).
  • Spares: +20 % on connectors and wire, plus Procedure C practice stock.

8. Open Items & Revision History

8.1 Open items (resolve before ordering)

  • ☐ Decision needed: GH contacts accept 26–30 AWG only, but CAN wire must be 22 AWG or thicker. Options: (a) solder a 22 AWG trunk to a short 26–28 AWG GH pigtail and protect the splice with adhesive heat shrink; (b) confirm the CAN plugs in our photos use a different crimp that takes 22 AWG. Check what is in the plug in the photo first.
  • ☐ Identify the black power connector (P2) and read the RS00/RS02/RS04 manuals (only RS03 was read)
  • ☐ Confirm the white CAN plugs are GH1.25 by measuring pitch with calipers
  • ☐ Confirm 16 AWG is adequate for peak current on RS03/RS04 segments
  • ☐ Confirm F–1 and F–6 feed routing from PDB/ESP32 CAN transceivers
  • ☐ Fill BOM prices at checkout (Amazon/DigiKey blocked automated reads); preview the videos in 6.1a
  • ☐ Confirm the motors have the CAN terminator built in (the manual does not say)

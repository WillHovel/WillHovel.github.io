Mini Humanoid Build Guide

The new-member project: ten members (6 electrical, 2 mechanical, 2 simulation/ML) build a stock Otto humanoid on SG90 servos, design and order one shared PCB, then split into two teams that each modify their own robot. I wrote the schedule, requirements and review gates so every number traces back to a measurement or a datasheet.

Overview

Ten new members build stock Otto together by Fri Oct 30 and a shared PCB by Nov 3, then split into two teams that each modify their own mini humanoid; both robots done Saturday Nov 28.

  • Team: 6 electrical (new Triton Droids members), 2 mechanical, 2 simulation/ML.
  • What you already know: each electrical member did either the Power Distribution Board breakdown or the Control Board breakdown. This project reuses both: fusing, bulk/decoupling caps, buck vs LDO, and current budgets from the PDB; ESP32-S3 boot/reset, USB-C, decoupling and connector placement from the Control Board.
  • Thanksgiving is Thu Nov 26. Plan as if the last week has only three working days.

The actuator: SG90 micro servo

The team is using the Beffkkip SG90 3-pack on Amazon. Typical SG90 ratings from a published SG90 spec sheet:

SpecTypical SG90 value
Weight9 g
Size22.2 × 11.8 × 31 mm
Stall torque1.8 kgf·cm (at 4.8 V)
Speed0.1 s / 60°
Supply4.8–6 V
Gears / rangePlastic, about 180°

Starting point: fork Otto DIY Humanoid

Don't start the CAD from scratch. Fork the open-source Otto DIY Humanoid, which already walks on SG90s, and spend the team's design time on upgrades instead.

  • Base CAD: Otto DIY Humanoid "the fat version" on Printables. It uses 6 micro servos (2 hips, 2 ankles, 2 arms), and the page links the Fusion 360 CAD source so Mechanical can edit parts, not just print STLs. Same part serves as leg and arm.
  • Smaller expansion version: Otto DIY Humanoid expansion, which bolts arms onto the basic biped; it takes about 3.5 h and 51 g of filament to print the expansion.
  • Assembly reference: Otto DIY Humanoid on Instructables (assembly steps, servo centering, Blockly/Arduino code).
  • License: Otto is CC-BY-SA, so credit Otto DIY and share any remixed CAD under the same license.

Why Otto and not a 16–17 DOF kit? The 17-DOF open humanoids we found (for example the Atom-X) are built around stronger bus servos, not SG90s. A 6-servo base walks on SG90 torque and gets a robot standing by mid-November; extra DOF is a stretch goal.

Modifications come later. Phase 1 builds stock Otto exactly as published. In Phase 2 each team picks its own upgrades (elbows, shoulder roll, head pan, an IMU, a PCB Rev B, a new gait). The club has 50+ SG90s on hand, so servo count is not a constraint; mass and torque are.

  • ☐ Hand the CSV format to Electrical so firmware can play it.

Phase 2 — two teams, own robots (Sat Nov 7 to Sat Nov 28)

Team A and Team B each run the same checklist on their own Otto. The only difference is the upgrade each team picks. Each team's lead tracks who owns what.

Week 3 — Sat Nov 7 to Fri Nov 13: pick upgrades, bring up the PCB

  • ☐ Team meeting Sat Nov 7: choose 1–2 upgrades (elbows, shoulder roll, head pan, IMU, better gait) and write a one-paragraph plan with a mass estimate.
  • ☐ Electrical: when boards arrive (typically 1–2 weeks after ordering, check your fab's quote), solder your team's copy and run the bring-up plan: rails with nothing attached, then MCU, then one servo, then all.
  • ☐ Electrical: move firmware from the dev board to the PCB; confirm every servo channel.
  • ☐ Mechanical: CAD the upgrade parts; print and test-fit.
  • ☐ Sim: add the upgrade joints to your team's URDF; check the robot still balances.
  • ☐ Electrical: decide whether the upgrade needs a PCB Rev B (more servo channels, IMU). If yes, write the delta requirements.

Done when (Fri Nov 13): your Otto walks on the team PCB; upgrade parts printed.

Week 4 — Sat Nov 14 to Fri Nov 20: build the upgrade

  • ☐ Mechanical + Electrical: install the upgrade on your Otto; wire new servos.
  • ☐ Electrical (optional second revision): schematic + layout review, order by Tue Nov 17; until it arrives, run extra servos from spare PCB headers or a breadboard.
  • ☐ Sim + Electrical: play the updated sim gait on the real robot; tune trims; note where sim and hardware disagree.
  • ☐ Whole team: record a video of the robot doing the new motion.

Done when (Fri Nov 20): upgraded robot moves under firmware.

Week 5 — Sat Nov 21 to Sat Nov 28: finish and demo

Thanksgiving is Thu Nov 26 — plan on Sat–Wed only.

  • ☐ Electrical: bring up Rev B if ordered; otherwise finalize wiring on Rev A.
  • ☐ Mechanical: mount battery and board, tidy wiring.
  • ☐ Whole team: full battery test run by Wed Nov 25.
  • ☐ Whole team: demo and a one-page write-up (what you changed, measured numbers, what broke) Sat Nov 28.

Fallback: a team's stock Otto on Rev A from Week 3 is always a working robot; an unfinished upgrade becomes the next project, not a missed deadline.

Electrical: PCB requirements

By Wed Oct 28 the electrical group writes a one-page PCB requirements doc answering every point below with a number or decision and one line of reasoning. Nobody owns a section: split it however you like, then everyone reads the whole doc before the review.

Design Rev A for stock Otto plus headroom, so teams can add servos and sensors in Phase 2 without a new board.

General requirements

  • Servo outputs: drive all 6 stock Otto servos plus spare channels for upgrades. Decide how many spares and justify it.
  • Servo power: a servo rail inside the SG90's 4.8–6 V range across the full battery range, sized for your measured peak current.
  • Logic power: a clean 3.3 V rail that does not brown out when every servo moves at once.
  • Battery: choose a battery and justify voltage range, weight, run time and charging.
  • Protection: input fuse or current limit, reverse-battery protection, bulk and decoupling capacitance.
  • Microcontroller: pick one with enough PWM outputs (or a PWM driver chip), USB programming, and boot/reset access.
  • Expansion: at least one spare I²C header for an IMU or other sensor in Phase 2.
  • Battery sensing: firmware can read battery voltage and stop before over-discharge.
  • Bring-up: test points on every rail, a power LED, and labeled connectors.
  • Fit: board outline, mounting holes and connector edges match Mechanical's sketch of the body.

Questions to answer (with what you already learned)

  • What is your measured stall current per servo, and what peak current do you design for? Do the fuse, regulator and trace widths all agree with that number? (PDB Q1.1, Q5.1)
  • LDO or buck for each rail, and how much heat would an LDO make at your current? (PDB Q2.1; Control Board AP2112K, Q1.3)
  • What happens to the logic rail when the servos start together, and where does bulk capacitance go? (PDB Q1.2; Control Board Q1.4)
  • How is a backwards battery handled? (Control Board Q1.2)
  • How many hardware PWM channels does your MCU have? Look it up in the datasheet; if short, compare direct PWM vs. an I²C PWM driver.
  • Is a 3.3 V PWM signal enough to drive the SG90's signal input? Test it in Week 1 rather than guessing.
  • Do servo ground and logic ground meet at one point? (PDB Q5.3)
  • How is the board programmed: USB-C direct to the MCU, boot and reset buttons? (Control Board Q2.3, Q3.2, Q3.3)
  • Pin map: every MCU pin, its net name and its job, agreed with whoever writes firmware before layout starts.

What leads check at each review

  • ☐ Every number traces back to a measurement or a datasheet page.
  • ☐ Peak current, fuse rating and regulator rating are consistent.
  • ☐ Spare servo channels and the expansion header are on the board.
  • ☐ Board outline fits Mechanical's sketch.
  • ☐ Every electrical member has contributed to the schematic or layout.

Leg Harness Rebuild: BOM, Cut List & Build Guide

This is the document that turned the inspection results into a rebuild: one wire standard, a priced and stock-checked BOM, a cut list derived from measured lengths, step-by-step build procedures for each connector, and eight acceptance checks. The robot is now fully wired from these builds.

Document TD-ELEC-PRC-002BOM $72.57 before shipping and taxHarnesses 10, to one standardAuthor Will Hovel, Electrical Lead
Reproduced from the team’s working document. The build log in Section 5.2 is the Oct 31 snapshot; all harnesses have since been completed and installed. Teammate names are replaced with Member numbers; links to team files are omitted.

All 10 leg harnesses are being rebuilt to one standard: 16 AWG red/black for power and 22 AWG twisted pair for CAN. The BOM comes to $72.57 before shipping and tax, and it covers every wire in the cut list except the two feed segments still to be measured.

3. Build Standard

Every harness is built the same way, so any member can inspect or repair any wire. Deviations need a lead’s sign-off, noted in the build log (Section 5).

3.2 Rules

  • Straight-through pinout: pin 1 at End X goes to pin 1 at End Y, and CAN_H goes to CAN_H. Never cross a pair.
  • Length: cut the power pair to the Section 2 length. Cut the CAN pair to match, then trim it so its connectors sit level with the power connectors.
  • Twist: keep the CAN pair twisted to within 2 cm of each connector or splice.
  • Flexibility: no rigid boot at any connector. Heat shrink covers only the joint and ≤ 5 mm of insulation, so the wire can still bend close to the housing (inspection test B4).
  • Sleeve: run both pairs in one 1/4 in braided sleeve. Stop the sleeve 3 cm short of each connector and lock each sleeve end with a 6.5 mm heat-shrink band.
  • Label: put a masking-tape flag 5 cm from End X with the Wire ID and “X” (e.g. “W-67 X”). Lab stock; it is not a BOM line.
  • No tape insulation: heat shrink only. Tape on a joint fails inspection.

4. Build Procedures

Build each harness in this order: cut, terminate End X, terminate End Y, sleeve, label, inspect (Section 5). Watch the videos for a connector before your first one; Section 4.5 lists them.

4.0 Setup (every harness)

  • Cut the power pair and CAN pair to the Section 2 length. Slide on the sleeve now if both ends use the same connector type, otherwise after End X.
  • Pre-load heat shrink on each wire: 3.7 mm for 16 AWG cups, 1.5 mm for 22 AWG cups and splices.
  • Iron at about 350–370 °C with a chisel tip for XT30 power cups. Use a fine tip for the small cups and splices.
  • Mate the plug with a spare male connector while soldering. It holds the pins aligned and stops the housing deforming from heat.

4.1 P1: Amass XT30UW-F (power)

  • Strip the 16 AWG red and black wires to the depth of the solder cup. Twist the strands and tin them.
  • Fill each cup about half full of solder.
  • Find pin 1 (+) from the housing’s polarity mark. Red goes to pin 1, black to pin 2.
  • Reheat the cup, push the tinned wire in until it bottoms out, and hold it still until the solder sets.
  • Slide the 3.7 mm heat shrink over each cup up to the housing, then shrink.
  • Check with inspection criteria W1–W6 (shiny concave fillet, no stray strands, no melted plastic).

4.2 P2: Amass XT30(2+2)-F.G.B (power + CAN, RS02 motors)

Pin map from the RS02 manual: 1 = +, 2 = −, 3 = CAN_L, 4 = CAN_H.

  • Terminate the signal contacts first, while there is room to reach them: green to pin 3, yellow to pin 4. The XT30(2+2) is an XT30 power block with crimped signal contacts, so crimp these with the PA-09 as in 4.3 steps 2–4 (the LCSC listing calls the part “solder type”, so check the contacts on the part you receive).
  • Solder the power cups as in 4.1, steps 1–5: red to pin 1, black to pin 2.
  • The supplier gives 16–20 AWG for the power cups and no size for the signal contacts. If the 22 AWG CAN wire does not fit a signal contact, stop. Use a 4 cm 26 AWG pigtail and splice it as in 4.3, steps 6–10.
  • Continuity-check all four pins against End X before sleeving.

4.3 S1: JST GH 1.25 mm (CAN, crimp + splice)

GH contacts take 26–30 AWG only, so the 22 AWG trunk cannot be crimped directly. Each S1 end is a short 26 AWG pigtail, crimped into the housing and soldered to the trunk.

Crimp the pigtail
  • Cut two 4 cm pieces of 26 AWG wire, one green and one yellow (BOM 5).
  • Strip just enough of one end to fill the contact’s conductor barrel. The insulation barrel must grip the jacket, not bare wire.
  • Crimp each contact (SSHL-002T-P0.2) with the PA-09 in the smallest die that closes fully: conductor barrel first, then insulation barrel.
  • Push each contact into the GHR-02V-S housing until it clicks. Tug it gently: it must not come out.
  • Match the pin order to the motor’s GH header (CAN_L / CAN_H). Use the same order at both ends of every harness. Check it against a known-good motor before the first build.
Splice to the 22 AWG trunk
  • Strip about 6 mm of the 26 AWG free end and of the 22 AWG trunk. Slide 1.5 mm heat shrink onto the trunk.
  • Lay the wires end to end, green to green and yellow to yellow, and solder them as an inline splice (SparkFun method, 4.5).
  • Stagger the two splices by about 1 cm so the bundle does not bulge.
  • Keep each splice 2–3 cm from the housing so the pigtail stays free to flex.
  • Shrink the heat shrink over each splice and continuity-check through the housing.

4.4 Sleeve and label

  • Pull the 1/4 in sleeve over both pairs. Stop it 3 cm short of each connector.
  • Lock each sleeve end with a 6.5 mm heat-shrink band. Melt any frayed sleeve ends lightly with the heat gun.
  • Add the End X label flag (rule 3.2.6).

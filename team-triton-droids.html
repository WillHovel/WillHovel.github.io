Triton Droids: Electrical Lead

Triton Droids is the UC San Diego team building a full-scale humanoid robot. We are building humanoid robots so that one day people don’t have to do unsustainable or dangerous manual jobs. I lead the electrical design: power, sensing and the control network. Our first milestone is a robot that kicks a soccer ball by January 2027.

Why

The reason I build robots

Some manual work is dangerous, and some of it is unsustainable for the people doing it. A humanoid can use tools and spaces built for people, which is why we are building one. My part is the electrical nervous system: I turn motor currents, bus traffic and rail voltages into signals I can see on a scope or in a log, so the team can tell what the robot is doing and why it fails.

My role

What I own

  • Power: I brought up 3 power distribution boards, adding current limiting.
  • Debugging: I isolate faults by correlating schematics to layout with an oscilloscope, DMM and signal injection.
  • Control: I designed the control board that links the limbs, head and torso over CAN.
  • Integration: I coordinate power, sensing and control integration with the machine learning and mechanical design sub-teams.
  • Training: I designed the self-balancing robot task and the Control Board Breakdown assignment new members work through in Week 1.
Work

Boards and builds

Control board

Bring-up and debugging

I ran continuity, power-up and rail tests on the power boards and isolate faults with a scope, DMM and signal injection.

Onboarding task: self-balancing robot

The soldering, wiring and system-design task I built to train 20+ new members.

Mini Aloha

Compact bimanual robot platform. I did the electrical integration: buck converter with live voltage/current display, CAN servo wiring and joystick firmware.

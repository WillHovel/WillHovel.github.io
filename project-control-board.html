Humanoid Control Board: Five CAN Buses from One ESP32-S3

I designed the control board for the Triton Droids humanoid robot. One ESP32-S3 talks to the motor controllers in four limbs and in the head and torso over five CAN buses, so the joints share two-wire networks instead of a tangle of PWM and serial lines. I drew the schematic, laid out the 4-layer board in KiCad, and exported the production files.

Problem

A robot with many joints needs a robust network

A humanoid has many joints, and every extra wire is weight, a failure point, and a debugging session. The robot is only worth building if it can take over work that is dangerous or unsustainable for people, so I wanted a controller that is simple to bring up and tolerant of the electrical noise from the motor drivers nearby.

CAN gives each limb one differential pair that rejects common-mode noise and lets me add nodes without adding pins.

Design

What I built

  • Controller: ESP32-S3-MINI-1 with USB-C straight to the chip for flashing, plus BOOT and reset buttons.
  • Limb buses (4): MCP2515 CAN controllers over SPI, two on each of two SPI buses, each with its own chip select and interrupt, feeding SN65HVD230 3.3 V transceivers.
  • Head/torso bus: the ESP32-S3’s built-in TWAI controller on GPIO17/18 into a fifth SN65HVD230.
  • Termination: a 120 Ω resistor behind a 2-pin jumper on every bus.
  • Clock: the ESP32 drives the MCP2515 clock inputs through a 33 Ω series resistor, so the four controllers share one source.
  • Power: 5 V in through an SS14 Schottky, an AP2112K-3.3 LDO, a power LED, and test pins for 5 V and 3.3 V.

Lessons

Design choices

  • The ESP32-S3 has one CAN (TWAI) controller and I needed five buses, so the limbs use MCP2515s that add controllers over SPI.
  • Splitting the four MCP2515s across two SPI buses keeps each bus lightly loaded.
  • Termination sits behind jumpers so a bus can be terminated or left open depending on where the board lands in the harness.

What I will check or change

  • LDO heat. At 5 V in, the AP2112K drops about 1.4 V, so the worst-case bound puts about 0.7 W in a SOT-23-5. I will check its temperature under CAN load at bring-up and move to a buck regulator if it runs hot.
  • USB lines. D+ and D− go straight to the connector. After the glove, I would add ESD protection and keep those traces short and matched.
  • Termination. I confirm 60 Ω across CANH/CANL with two jumpers on before connecting any motor.

Methods and hardware files

Validation plan: how I will measure each row

Before power

I check continuity and shorts between 5 V, 3.3 V and ground, and measure 120 Ω on each termination with the jumper in.

Power-up

I apply 5 V from a current-limited bench supply and record input current, 3.3 V rail voltage and LDO temperature.

Firmware

I flash over USB, then run each MCP2515 in loopback mode and the TWAI controller in self-test, so every controller works before I add a second node.

Bus

I connect two nodes with both terminations on, confirm 60 Ω across CANH/CANL with the board off, run 500 kbps and 1 Mbps while I watch CANH/CANL on a scope, and log error counters for 10 minutes.
